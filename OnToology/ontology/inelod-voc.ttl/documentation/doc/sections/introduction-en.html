Introduction back to ToC

The Spanish National Statistics Institute (Instituto Nacional de Estadística, INE) is the autonomous body, attached to the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Enterprise, in charge of the general coordination of the statistical services of the General State Administration in Spain and of the production of statistics for state purposes, as established by Law 12/1989 of 9 May, on the Public Statistical Function. Its output includes operations as diverse as the continuous population register (padrón continuo), the population and housing census, the national accounts, the price indices, the labour force survey or the death statistics, to mention just a few.

These operations generate large volumes of statistical data structured as multidimensional tables or data cubes, in which each observation is located by a set of dimensions (what is observed: time, territory, sex, age group, economic sector, etc.) and quantified by one or more measures (the observed numerical value: number of births, euros, index, rate, etc.). This conceptual organisation comes from the multidimensional model consolidated in OLAP systems and formalised, for statistical data, in the international standard SDMX (Statistical Data and Metadata Exchange), published as ISO 17369 and maintained by the main international statistical institutions (BIS, ECB, Eurostat, IMF, OECD, UN and World Bank; see sdmx.org).

With the aim of publishing these cubes as Linked Data following the principles set out by Berners-Lee (2006), the W3C approved in January 2014 the RDF Data Cube Vocabulary (QB) as an official Recommendation, aligned with the SDMX information model and specifically designed to represent multidimensional statistical data in RDF. QB defines an abstract core (qb:DataSet, qb:DataStructureDefinition, qb:ComponentSpecification, qb:Observation, qb:DimensionProperty, qb:MeasureProperty, qb:AttributeProperty) but, deliberately, leaves the concrete definition of the dimension and measure properties applicable to each domain in the hands of every data producer.

This document describes INELOD VOC, the vocabulary developed within the INELOD project to cover that specific layer: an ontology that declares, in a harmonised and reusable way, the concrete dimension and measure properties used in the RDF data cubes derived from INE's statistical operations. The official description registered in the ontology itself by means of dct:description reads: «An ontology to describe the variables used as dimensions and measures in the RDF data cubes of the Instituto Nacional de Estadística».

Purpose and scope of the vocabulary

The purpose of INELOD VOC is to provide a controlled, unique and unambiguous set of identifiers (URIs) and RDF properties for all variables acting as dimensions or measures in the cubes published by INE as Linked Data. Three concrete benefits follow from this:

  • Semantic interoperability across INE's own statistical operations: a given variable (e.g., sex, age or nationality) is represented by the same RDF property regardless of the survey or register of origin.
  • Traceability and international reuse: specific properties are modelled, whenever possible, as rdfs:subPropertyOf of the dimensions and measures standardised in sdmx-dimension and sdmx-measure, enabling federated queries with other producers that also reuse SDMX in RDF (W3C Government Linked Data WG; Cyganiak et al.).
  • Compliance with the European High-Value Datasets framework defined by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2023/138, which includes statistical and demographic categories for which the RDF Data Cube model is the natural semantic representation.

The thematic scope of INELOD VOC is deliberately transversal to all areas of INE's statistical production. Among the families of properties incorporated in the vocabulary are those relating to demography (births, deaths, marriages, divorces, age groups, generations, countries and nationalities), economy and national accounts (GDP, aggregates, institutional sectors, balance of payments, accounting transactions), labour market (employment status, NACE branches, EPA), prices (CPI and sub-indices), education (CNED levels, fields of study), health (causes of death, healthcare, birth weight), agriculture (holdings, crops, livestock, holder's training), justice (civil and criminal cases, judgments), tourism (excursions, overnight stays, average length of stay), environment (emissions, waste, water) and units of measurement (euros, hectares, kilograms, hectolitres, kilotonnes, etc.).
